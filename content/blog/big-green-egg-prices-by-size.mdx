## What's Included with Every Big Green Egg?

Every new Big Green Egg comes with the ceramic cooker, a stainless steel cooking grid, a fire grate, a fire ring, a dual-function metal top (draft door), and a ceramic damper top. That's enough to get you grilling on day one.

What's not included, and this surprises people, is a nest or table to put it in. The EGG itself doesn't come with legs. You'll need one of these:

- **IntEGGrated Nest + Handler**: $200 - $350 depending on size. This is the most popular option. Gives you a rolling cart with folding side shelves.
- **Modular Nest System**: $150 - $250. A simple stand with casters.
- **EGG Frame**: $400 - $600. A steel table frame that holds the EGG at counter height.
- **Custom Table**: $800 - $2,000+. Wood or composite tables built specifically for the EGG.

I always tell customers to budget an extra $300 to $500 beyond the EGG price for a nest and your first round of accessories. That puts a realistic Large setup at $1,600 to $2,000 all-in.

## EGGcessory Starter Kits

Big Green Egg offers starter kit bundles that save you 10-15% compared to buying pieces individually. For a Large, the most popular kit runs about $200 and includes:

- convEGGtor (plate setter for indirect cooking)
- Ash tool
- Grid gripper
- Internal temperature gauge upgrade

I recommend every single customer buy the convEGGtor from day one. Without it, you can't do indirect cooking, which means no smoking, no baking, no pizza. It's the single most important accessory.

### What About the EGG Genius?

The EGG Genius is Big Green Egg's wifi-enabled temperature controller. It runs about $250 and lets you monitor and adjust your cook from your phone. For overnight brisket smokes or low-and-slow pork shoulders, it's a game changer. You can set your target temp, go to bed, and the fan system holds it steady all night. I don't consider it essential for beginners, but most serious cooks add one within the first year.

## Which Size Should You Buy?

Here's what I tell people in the showroom after 50 years in retail:

**Buy the Large** if you're a typical family of 3-6, you entertain occasionally, and you want one cooker that does everything. This is the right answer for 80% of customers.

**Buy the XLarge** if you regularly cook for 8+ people, you want to smoke multiple racks of ribs at once, or you're the designated cookout host for your neighborhood. Plenty of folks in the Hamburg or Chevy Chase neighborhoods come in wanting the Large, then realize they're hosting UK game day cookouts for 15 people every fall Saturday and size up.

**Buy the Medium** if you're cooking for 1-2 people most of the time and space is tight. It's still a fantastic cooker.

**Buy the MiniMax** if you want portability. It's the same cooking area as the Small but designed to travel. I've sold dozens of these to UK tailgaters who haul them to Kroger Field every Saturday.
